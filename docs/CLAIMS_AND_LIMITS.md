# Evidence classification and scientific boundary

| Claim | Status | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solar and tidal forcing, ocean induction, solid-Earth loading, porous flow | Established independently | Standard references and measured source data |
| Local tidal/stress-driven pore-pressure response | Observed under suitable conditions | Verify particular reservoir and sensor transfer function |
| Pressure-sensitive permeability and hysteretic transport | Physically plausible locally | Rock-specific calibration, reproducible parameter ranges |
| Proposed GRM H-04 for a natural field setting | **Unconfirmed** | Direct time-mean flux, negative controls, conservation, replication |
| GRM causes excess heat transport H-05 | **Unconfirmed** | Direct heat-flow measurement with power closure |
| Planet-wide petroleum cooling circuit | **Unsupported** | No current empirical or energy evidence |
| Extraction causes substantial warming by reducing petroleum cooling | **Unsupported** | No demonstrated causal/quantitative pathway |

The benchmark's pressure law `k(p)=k0 exp(beta*p)` is **assumed** in a finite range; it is not a discovered universal law. The open right boundary and periodically driven left boundary exchange fluid with reservoirs; those external reservoirs can provide work and a non-zero mean. With sealed/no-flux boundaries there cannot be sustained net boundary throughflow. The analytical Bessel result validates the code's solution for those boundary assumptions, **not** nature.

The reported thermal screen uses mean flux and fixed endpoint temperatures. It is not a fully coupled time-dependent heat/multi-phase/poroelastic simulation. Observed trends from distinct physical channels must not be conflated with a single unexplained energy force. Correlation, coherence, and index performance never substitute for causality.
